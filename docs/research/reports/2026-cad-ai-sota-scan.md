# CAD's paper surge outruns its proofs

**Status:** survey note, 2026-11-25. External work, not our result. Window: 25 August – 25
November 2026; arXiv-first, with vendor claims separated from independent measurement. The
per-paper evidence notes are local working material and are not committed; every claim below
carries an arXiv ID or a primary link. Exempt from the project glossary: this report quotes the
vocabulary of the fields it summarises.

The state of the art in AI for CAD as of 25 November 2026 is not a model that designs parts. It is an agent that proposes a program, a deterministic kernel that executes it, and a verifier that decides whether the result is engineering-correct — and the verifier is where the intellectual action moved. The window from 25 August to 25 November produced **199 arXiv papers** matching CAD in the abstract, against **109** in the same 93 days a year earlier (**+82.6%**) and **89** two years earlier (**2.2×**), yet NeurIPS 2026's main track accepted **four** CAD papers and none of its 125 workshops is CAD-dedicated ([arXiv API](https://export.arxiv.org/api/query), retrieved 2026-11-25). Independent measurement is brutal and internally consistent: **61.03/100** overall on CAD Bench V3, **17.5%** task success against an **87.0%** expert reference on CADWorld, **3.6%** success on cross-application engineering work in EngiWorld, and roughly **one-third** success on instruction-guided editing across three unrelated studies. Four results are genuinely new: hierarchical reward grouping that stops geometric and physical criteria compensating for each other (PhyCAD), constraint rewards extracted from generated code (ConCAD), a decay theory explaining why IoU and Chamfer are blind to local features together with a metric and ground-truth-free verifier built from it (DrawScore), and a 27B CAD-native model that reaches frontier parity and then adds the first credible verifier-scaling gain (VisCAD). Everything else in the surge is variation on those themes, and the largest category — near-duplicate text-to-CAD program synthesis — is accumulating engineering, not knowledge.

## The frontier is adjudication, not generation

Read the 199 papers as a distribution and the shape is unmistakable. Roughly **45%** are LLM or code-generation papers emitting CadQuery, FreeCAD Python or OpenSCAD, **~20%** are agent and tool-use harnesses, **~16%** are geometry-native learning on B-reps and equivariant graphs, **~20%** are applications, **~9%** are evaluation and **~5%** are surveys, with rows overlapping because an agent paper is also a code-generation paper. The mix is shifting away from geometry-native research toward LLM-shaped research, and that shift explains most of the volume.

What changed qualitatively is the objective function. Pre-window work optimised executability plus Chamfer or IoU. In this window the reward is increasingly something the kernel can adjudicate: extracted B-rep constraints ([ConCAD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34494)), finite-element feasibility ([PhyCAD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09401)), projected engineering-drawing error ([DrawScore](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24681)), a symbolic constraint tree ([CIT-CAD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07434)), or a reference-free structural check ([GOPR-CAD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11470)). That is a real maturation: the field stopped treating a CAD file as an image and started treating it as an artifact with a validity predicate attached.

But the predicate is weak. A frontier agent executes **93.11%** of industrial part tasks and still scores conditional surface IoU **0.1393** ([RealCADBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03773)); a CAD-native 27B beats the best frontier VLM by **0.0044** on a composite profile, a margin inside rubric noise ([VisCAD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03811)). The consistent reading is that **the model is a proposal generator and the kernel is the oracle**, and that system quality is bounded by validation coverage rather than parameter count. Autodesk's research blog says it plainly about its shipped feature: the model is "not recovering history, proposing a plausible one," and candidates are accepted only after re-execution and geometric checking ([Autodesk Research](https://www.research.autodesk.com/blog/rebuilding-design-history-with-autotimeline/)).

Where the hype sits follows. The agentic-assistant narrative — an AI that decides what to design — is demo-only at every vendor in the window; shipped features are bounded, checkable transformations. The generative narrative is contradicted by the only independent leaderboard, which puts whole-system authoring at 61/100 with create-and-edit at **39.69**. The "CAD foundation model" narrative is, so far, a 27B fine-tune with a ~5% relative gain from reranking. None of that is nothing, but all of it is less than the press releases.

## 199 papers, four NeurIPS acceptances: the surge outruns the pipeline

The headline series was measured once, deliberately, before the index stopped answering. The query matched the abstract field on `"CAD" OR "computer-aided design" OR "computer aided design"` with a `submittedDate` range, count read from `opensearch:totalResults`: **199** for 2026-08-25→2026-11-25, **109** for the identical 2025 span, **89** for 2024 ([arXiv API](https://export.arxiv.org/api/query), 15:24 UTC 2026-11-25). Arithmetic on measured counts gives **+82.6%** year on year, **+123.6%** against 2024, and a two-year CAGR of **49.5%**.

Three caveats bound what that means. About ten minutes after capture, `export.arxiv.org` began returning **HTTP 429** for every request from the research host for the remaining ~50 minutes, so the counts **could not be re-confirmed**. An independent Semantic Scholar bulk count over the same windows — 631 retrieved, 195 dropped as medical "CAD", **234** AI-matching — grows only **+21%** (234 vs 193 vs 160), but its denominator *falls* in the newest window (631 vs 699 vs 712), the signature of indexing lag, so +21% is a floor. And a stricter classifier requiring a CAD term in the *title* yields **156** instead of 234, a ~33% swing from one coding decision; every S2-derived count below should be read as ±⅓.

The growth is lopsided. **Text-to-CAD and program synthesis** went 20→35→**63** (3.2× vs 2024), **agents** 11→13→**47** (4.3×), **B-rep and geometry-native learning** 17→29→**37** (2.2×), **assembly** 7→6→**22**, **surveys** 10→13→**31**, **drawings** 18→18→**31**, and **manufacturing/CAM/DFM** 35→47→**50** (1.4×). Loose evaluation vocabulary went 88→122→**181**, though that row is inflated by "evaluat*" appearing anywhere; the hand-coded count of papers that are actually a benchmark, dataset or metric contribution is **~20–24**, and a secondary census counts **29 dedicated CAD benchmarks since 2024, 16 of them released between April and September 2026** ([CDFAM](https://cdfam.com/ai-to-cad-benchmarks-2026/), itself LLM-compiled and non-peer-reviewed).

The sharpest number is a near-zero: a whole-corpus phrase search for `"CAD question answering" | "CAD VQA" | "CAD understanding"` returns **1 / 1 / 3** papers in the 2026 / 2025 / 2024 windows. CAD evaluation here is execution- and artifact-based — does the STEP rebuild, is it watertight, does it pass DFM — not VQA-shaped. The few VQA-flavoured papers are about *drawings*, not solid models. That absence is the strongest single signal about what the field optimises, and it is why the evaluation story below is about kernels and tolerance bands rather than question sets.

Then the venue check, which reframes the series. NeurIPS 2026's main track contains **four** CAD papers out of 678 unique candidates screened across ten CAD terms, and **none of its 125 workshops** is dedicated to CAD, mechanical design or geometry-for-manufacturing. CAD'26 accepted **27 papers total**, of which **5** are CAD-generation-focused and **9** contain any AI. The Elsevier journal *Computer-Aided Design* ran **35** articles with **10** AI-titled. The one new piece of venue infrastructure is a *workshop*: ECCV 2026's "The Path to Manufacturing," held 8 September, with a Render+TechDraw→STEP challenge on 8,344 training and 927 hidden test items ([workshop site](https://3dgen-cad-workshop.github.io/)).

Together those facts explain the surge without appeal to a boom in accepted science. A **+80% jump in postings cannot come from venues that accept four to twenty-seven CAD papers a year.** It is preprints, produced by groups who can now generate a paper with a frozen frontier model, a CadQuery harness and a GRPO script, and whose publication pipeline is thinner than the posting rate implies. The count is fragile even at retrieval: of 631 Semantic Scholar CS records matching "CAD", **195 (31%) were medical** (coronary artery disease, computer-aided diagnosis, CAD/CAM dentistry), and EDA plus USD/CAD add two more homonyms. Every figure here is abstract-term-matched on the mechanical sense or explicitly filtered.

## What the surge is made of: thirty-five variations on one paper

The clearest evidence that volume is outrunning novelty is lexical. In one 93-day window there are roughly **35 distinct papers** whose titles follow `<Method>-CAD: <verb> CAD from <modality>` — ExpConCAD, PrimitiveCAD, GraphCAD, GOPR-CAD, CIT-CAD, IterCAD, ReliCAD, ConCAD, CAD-WAM, PhyCAD, CADFather, CADMAGEDDON, VGGT-CAD and two dozen more. This is naming and framing convergence, not measured textual overlap: no similarity analysis was run, so the claim is distributional rather than statistical. But the shared recipe is legible — a frozen or lightly fine-tuned VLM emits CadQuery or FreeCAD Python, a kernel executes it, a repair loop runs, and the reward is executability plus IoU/Chamfer plus one constraint or physics term, evaluated on DeepCAD, Fusion 360, MCB or BenchCAD.

CadQuery's dominance is itself a finding about why the surge happened. IterCAD generates CadQuery from dimensioned orthographic views ([2608.24020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24020)); CIT-CAD builds a Constraint Intent Tree as scaffold and verification target ([2609.07434](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07434)); Intent2CAD lifts supervision from primitive calls to semantic features ([2611.03449](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03449)). FreeCAD Python is the second dialect. B-rep-native generation is a smaller, separate track — UniBRep fits surfaces into a kernel from a feature mesh ([2610.04092](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04092)), GraphCAD diffuses over edge-adjacency graphs ([2610.30974](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30974)) — and no one proposes B-rep *tokens* as a competitive representation for LLM generation. The token-versus-code debate has settled toward code for program synthesis and graphs or meshes for direct geometry, which is what made cheap participation possible: a text-to-CAD paper now needs a Python interpreter and an API key, not a geometry pipeline.

Sorting genuinely new from incremental produces a short first list. **New:** reward hierarchies instead of reward sums — PhyCAD shows a flat GRPO objective *raises* validity to 98.40% while *lowering* joint feasibility from 21.76% to 18.80%, a finding about multi-objective RL for verifiable engineering, not a CAD trick; constraint extraction as a dense, execution-free reward — ConCAD's ablation shows kernel-gated rewards cap at 0.702 IoU while code-level constraints reach 0.734; a falsifiable theory of metric blindness plus a metric and verifier built from it; a CAD-native model with verifier scaling; and artifact-checked computer-use benchmarks with very low absolute scores. **Incremental:** more agent loops with a VLM judge, better tokenisation, more operations per program, larger synthetic corpora, graph diffusion over B-reps (a 2022–2024 lineage), and domain fine-tunes where the difference is data curation and reranking rather than architecture.

The same benchmark also yields different numbers in different papers — CADFather is 0.987 on full DeepCAD and 0.980 on a 300-part subset — so only within-paper rows are comparable. A reader who aggregates these into a ranking has invented a number no paper reports.

## IoU integrated away the errors engineers care about

If the window has one methodological centre, it is that the dominant metrics were shown to be structurally blind, analytically rather than anecdotally. Three results do it, and a fourth shows the headline validity number means less than it appears.

### The decay law behind five years of inflated scores

DrawScore formalises volumetric IoU, Chamfer distance and surface F-score as instances of one integral and shows that a local error of size *s* carries weight decaying as *s* to the power **(k+q)**, where *k* is the dimension of the integration domain and *q* comes from the penalty function ([arXiv:2611.24681](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24681), 18 November). For a volumetric integral the exponent is large, so a misplaced hole is nearly free. The paper validates the predicted decay orders on controlled perturbations — fitted slopes within 0.2 of *k+q* — and shows the consequence: a **0.9 mm bolt-hole shift leaves surface F-score at 100% while DrawScore drops to 95.94%**, and **1,188 valid outputs** across six model-agent configurations exist where IoU rates the result near-perfect while projected-curve error exposes a misplaced hole or a slot on the wrong face.

The constructive half matters as much. Projecting both models into orthographic views with visible and hidden lines and applying a saturating penalty along projected curves yields only **linear** decay, matching the intuition that a wrong hole is a wrong part. Because the projection comes from the drawing rather than the ground-truth solid, the score doubles as an **RL reward and a test-time verifier without 3D ground truth** — the only ground-truth-free verifier in the window, and the mechanism behind years of IoU above 0.9 on visibly broken parts.

### DeepCAD rewards the prior, not the perception

RealCAD shows that DeepCAD's local normalisation concentrates several geometric parameters on a few discrete values and hides scale in a single factor, so a model can post high **parameter accuracy** by emitting frequent values rather than inferring geometry ([arXiv:2608.30617](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30617), 31 August). Its own fix improves command and parameter accuracy by **+7.49 and +14.73 percentage points** over CADCrafter on real images **without** a corresponding mCD gain, and with a *higher* invalid ratio, because a few topology-defining parameters dominate the final shape. That is the cleanest statement in the window that token-level metrics are misaligned with the objective, and it is the only explicit benchmark-validity finding; no paper audited pretraining-corpus overlap with the ABC/DeepCAD/Fusion splits.

### Re-partitioning the same solid breaks the encoder

The same solid can be encoded by different B-reps — different operation order, a kernel rebuild, a different face partition, a different exporter — and existing B-rep encoders "collapse catastrophically" under such perturbations, including on a FreeCAD human dataset where different designers modelled the same part ([arXiv:2609.11573](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11573), 10 September). The proposed canonical region graph derives nodes, features and coordinate frame from the solid itself and is stable under every tested perturbation while matching the strongest baseline.

The implication is uncomfortable for much of the B-rep literature: **any benchmark that samples one representation per solid is measuring representation luck alongside geometry**, and models may be keying on encoding artefacts rather than shape. SO(3)-equivariant message passing helps — UV-Net drops to **9.22%** accuracy under rotation while the equivariant model holds 97.03% ([arXiv:2611.05449](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05449)) — but invariance is solved per paper rather than at the benchmark level.

### "Valid" means the kernel said yes

The fourth result should be quoted in every abstract that reports a validity percentage. A mathematics preprint of 13 November defines a *realization* of a tolerant B-rep as a piecewise-linear embedding of its cell complex inside the tolerance zones, proves uniqueness and existence conditions, and then shows that **in OpenCascade 8.0.1 the standard validity checks decide none of these questions**: a sewn wedge passes both checks yet has no realization, and a thick pipe that passes both has two realizations whose solids are not homeomorphic ([DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24203268](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24203268)).

So the headline rates are neither commensurable nor certificates. UniBRep reports **80.49%** valid B-reps on standard DeepCAD with face Chamfer 0.0345 versus 0.1096 for CADDreamer; GraphCAD reports **96.58%** validity on EAGBench-FS versus 77.45% for DiGress; HiFi-BRep, posted 17 August and therefore just outside the window, reports 72.20%. Each uses its own kernel version, split and definition. Validity became a training objective rather than a post-processing afterthought, and then the metric underneath it was shown to be a kernel-acceptance test.

Silent failure completes the picture. With 13 specification tasks mapped to machine-checkable B-rep properties and tolerance bands, four models from four vendors, and 156 attempts per architecture, agents **claimed completion on 98% of single-shot attempts and were silently wrong on 14.1%**, with stated confidence miscalibrated at **ECE 0.32** ([arXiv:2611.10063](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10063), 6 November). A measure-then-claim loop cut silent failures to **3.2%** and raised claim precision from **64% to 96%**, and the ablation attributes the gain to the *claim contract*, not the feedback. The oracle itself needed hardening — 18 of 519 verdicts flipped — and a blind expert audit overturned 3 of the 18 flagged failures, so even this number carries a ±.

## 61.03, 17.5, and 3.6: the measurement layer contradicts the launch deck

**CAD Bench V3** scores native FreeCAD artifacts with a programmatic verifier over 100 tasks — 30 text→CAD, 30 create-and-edit, 40 drawing→part — using mean continuous reward rather than binary success. In the 24 September snapshot over 13 model-agent systems, Claude Opus 5.5 with Claude Code leads at **61.03 overall, 50.87 on create-from-text, 39.69 on create-and-edit, and 84.66 on image-to-CAD** ([cadbench.ai](https://cadbench.ai/cad-bench/news/cad-bench-v3-opus-5-5); paper [arXiv:2611.02659](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02659)). The internal spread is the finding: **converting an existing drawing or image is comparatively tractable; authoring an editable parametric part from text, and editing it correctly, is not.**

**CADWorld** replaces "does it look right" with executable checks on saved FreeCAD files across 200 tasks and 11 workflow categories. The strongest of seven agents reaches **17.5%** against an **87.0%** expert reference ([arXiv:2609.16251](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16251), 14 September). Weaker agents never save an artifact; stronger ones save a readable project and then fail document structure, geometry and construction process. Failure migrates downstream as capability rises, so guardrails should target structure, not existence.

**EngiWorld** spans 1,301 expert-curated tasks across CAD, CAE, CAM, BIM, EDA and 3D visualisation on 26 platforms, graded by verifiers for geometric validity, physical feasibility and rule compliance. Best score **44.3/100** (a second research pass read 44.7; the discrepancy is unresolved and the honest value is ~44), and **only 3.6% of attempts succeed** when work must cross applications — 6 of 168 ([arXiv:2609.37686](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37686)).

**RealCADBench** supplies the industrial-intent version: on 1,770 tasks drawn from 12,632 industrial requests across 19 categories, nine models span **executability 0.565–0.812, conditional Solid IoU 0.2841–0.5379 and conditional Surface IoU 0.112–0.217** ([arXiv:2609.03773](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03773), 3 September). GPT-5.5 executes 93.11% with conditional surface IoU 0.1393; Kimi K3 has the best IoU yet executes only 34.22%. No model leads all four metrics.

| Benchmark | What it grades | Best measured | Headroom |
|---|---|---|---|
| CAD Bench V3 (02 Nov) | Native FreeCAD artifacts, 100 tasks | 61.03 overall; 39.69 create+edit | Large; edit is the weak slice |
| RealCADBench (03 Sep) | Industrial intents → FreeCAD programs | Exec 0.565–0.812; Surface IoU 0.112–0.217 | Very large |
| CADWorld (14 Sep) | GUI computer use in FreeCAD, 200 tasks | 17.5% vs 87.0% expert | Very large |

The asymmetry across that table is the most decision-relevant pattern in the report. **Benchmarks that score rendered or textual artifacts approach ceiling; benchmarks that score persistent editable state, constraints, or multi-tool workflows do not.** Drawing *understanding* is above 90% on clean single-sheet civil drawings — and 52.65% on large-format multi-domain drawings needing cross-region evidence, the honest ceiling for "reading". Drawing file *structure* sits at 60%, design at 48%, GUI work at 17.5%, cross-application work at 3.6%. Saturation is a property of the artifact you chose to score, not of the field.

Two results cut against that picture, for different reasons. CADMAGEDDON reconstructs editable CAD from meshes as a chain of simple visual decisions using a mid-sized Qwen3.8-27B model on one GPU, reporting **0.999 / 0.993 / 0.933** IoU on DeepCAD, Fusion and MCB 300-part subsets against 0.747 for the best frontier model on BenchCAD ([arXiv:2611.22563](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22563)). VisCAD trains a 27B suite on ~1M executed CadQuery programs to **0.5540** average part-level score versus 0.5496 for Gemini-3.1-Pro, then **0.5797** by reusing itself as a reranker ([arXiv:2609.03811](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03811)) — the first credible verifier-scaling result, with 0.0044 inside rubric noise.

Autodesk's Assistant became generally available in Fusion and is live in Inventor, Moldflow and Vault, with three MCP servers and a metered Flex-token allowance — evidence that inference cost is material at CAD scale and that AI is being sold as consumption rather than as a seat feature. AutoTimeline is a **tech preview**, and its availability statements conflict: the 22 October product update says all Fusion Design Extension users, while the 2 November research blog says the Insider Program with general availability "soon." Both are recorded; the discrepancy is unresolved. Dassault shipped SOLIDWORKS 2027 SP0 on 20 November with prompt, image and drawing to geometry, B-rep to parametric conversion and natural-language macros. Siemens shipped an NX copilot with specialised agents, search and an MCP endpoint in June, updated 18 November, and its CAM copilot is explicitly documentation and navigation rather than toolpath authoring.

The startups are more interesting because their architectures are visible. Backflip shipped 2D-drawing and phone-photo to editable 3D CAD on 17 September. WOGO's Hacadly, available 5 November, does drawing→STEP and history-less STEP→editable features inside the customer's own CAD, with the LLM on-device or in a customer-managed cloud — a direct answer to the data-privacy objection. Navier's Prism, released 3 November, runs Open CASCADE with twelve solver families, and its blog states the team concluded its own physics-ML models did not generalise and switched to real OpenFOAM, CalculiX and code_aster: the propose-then-verify thesis arrived commercially. Zoo shipped incremental Design Studio releases with no new model launch.

The largest event was financial. **Schneider Electric agreed on 4–5 October to acquire PTC for $205/share in cash — roughly $22.6bn equity, $23.7bn enterprise value, a 42.3% premium** ([PTC](https://www.ptc.com/en/news/2026/schneider-electric-to-acquire-ptc); [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/frances-schneider-electric-nears-20-billion-deal-buy-us-software-group-ptc-ft-2026-10-04/)). The buyer is an automation and energy company, not a CAD company, and it is simultaneously acquiring Cognite; the read is that design data is being positioned as the substrate for plant and factory AI rather than as a standalone product category. Against that, **no CAD-AI startup funding round was confirmed inside the window** — Backflip's $30M Series A (December 2024), Zoo's $30M (February 2025), Adam's $4.1M (October 2025) and MecAgent's $3M (May 2025) all predate it.

Open weights are not yet a substitute. In-window releases are 1.5B–32B research fine-tunes with zero to a few hundred downloads — the most visible, CAD-WAM at 1.79B under MIT, had **144** — with no permissively licensed model above 32B and no frontier-lab CAD foundation model. The moat is not weights; it is proprietary parametric corpora, kernel execution and validation harnesses, exactly what CAD Bench had to build. Autodesk's Q3 FY27 release (24 November, revenue $2.16bn, +17%) cites a consultancy pilot with **>90% less drawing-generation time**; it is vendor-reported and unreproduced, as are WOGO's 23/23 and Siemens' 70–80% automation target. The only independent artefact in this section is the leaderboard, and it says the ceiling is mid-range.

## Downstream, progress is wherever a simulator can grade the answer

The pattern separating credible downstream results from demos is whether a deterministic process can score the output. Where one can, numbers are real and often good; where the only judge is a VLM or a human checklist, they are low and contested.

**Drawings** are the clearest split stack. Extraction is close to solved in narrow trained settings — numerical content including GD&T frames at 0.963 F1 on a January baseline ([arXiv:2510.21862](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.21862), pre-window) — and generalist models read printed content at 89.7–95.3% with 91.7–95.5 F1 ([CivilCADBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28853), 21 November). But only **~62% of correctly placed points land on the correct CAD layer** in that same study; layer assignment overall is ~60% and design 48%, and a counting probe tops out at **59.3%** ([arXiv:2611.29309](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29309)). A study of 3,540 generations over 20 drawings found richer prompting did **not** improve identification accuracy while *unsupported claims* varied by nearly an order of magnitude across prompt architectures, with a text-only judge inflating fabrication **3–4×** ([DOI 10.3390/technologies14100666](https://doi.org/10.3390/technologies14100666)). The implication is per-clause verification, not trust.

**Assemblies** have been redefined from "generate an assembly" to "recover the constraint graph," because only the graph is editable, diffable and simulatable. Mate prediction from unassembled B-rep parts reaches **74.79%** constraint accuracy on a six-assembly set and **64.09%** on sixteen, rising to 81.78% and 80.93% with ground-truth faces ([arXiv:2610.14633](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14633)) — a proposal generator, not an unattended solver. FunCADBench makes function the metric across 46 mechanism families, graded by candidate-specific physical tests, and finds assemblies that look right and fail mechanically ([arXiv:2610.26861](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26861)). MUSE's negative result is the one to remember: high execution success does not imply correct assembly structure. RealCADBench's 25-task study shows the scaffolding trade — Codex with GPT-5.5 improves executability and both IoU metrics while *lowering* the rubric judge by **6.98 points**.

**Editing** is where the gap is widest and best measured, and three studies triangulate it. 3DCodeEdit runs 2,592 instruction-guided edits over 108 Blender programs and 17 models: average success **37%**, with even the best models below **60%** on structural and style edits, and failures shifting toward geometric foresight — the code runs but builds the wrong shape ([arXiv:2610.38231](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38231)). HistCAD reports edit success of **52.4% academic and 29.0% industrial** across 180,495 histories ([arXiv:2602.19171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19171), v1 February, baseline). CADWorld's 17.5% covers the same ground through a GUI. All three attribute failure to structure — history, constraints, foresight — rather than geometry, which makes "roughly one-in-three" cross-validated rather than a single-benchmark artefact. The cheapest credible intervention in the literature is the claim contract that cut silent failures 14.1%→3.2% with no model change.

**Simulation** produced the window's strongest technical result. CANTO tokenises NURBS patches — control points, knot vectors, weights — and maps continuous CAD geometry to surface and volume fields with no meshing of the input, scoring **19.8% lower surface-pressure relative L2 than AB-UPT** on HiLiftAeroML; because it is differentiable with respect to CAD parameters it finds designs with **4.4–20.4% lower drag** than the best dataset designs under matched constraints, **verified with the original CFD setup** ([arXiv:2609.36806](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36806)). Close behind is the ~1,650-parameter stent redesign: GPT-6 Astra runs 15 nonlinear FEA solves per cycle, lowers a high-percentile bend-strain objective by **32%** where the commercial optimiser Tosca improved ~10% and stalled, and an independent higher-fidelity model still shows **14% less strain and 63% less damage** ([arXiv:2611.26003](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26003)). Simulation *setup* is further along than replacement: agents produce executable PDE specifications with **98.3% correct first-turn outcomes** and zero false-ready decisions among 42 requests needing clarification or rejection ([arXiv:2610.41267](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41267)).

**Manufacturing** is the most credible deployment surface because the harness is the product. MillBill separates machining decisions from geometric execution: a locally hosted Qwen3.8-27B proposes operations and cutting parameters for 3-axis indexed work, a harness generates toolpaths and simulates removal, and the score is target surface within tolerance — **97.4% at τ=0.1 mm**, with 0.54% gouge against 1.50% for a GPT-6 agent ([arXiv:2611.22565](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22565)). Geo2Gcode compiles mesh to G-code through structured machining primitives and **machined one part on a physical CNC mill** ([arXiv:2611.20092](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20092)). STAMP makes formability a property of the representation — panels are depth maps over a draw plane, so undercuts are excluded by construction — and its FEA-gap predictor achieves **0.18 mm versus 1.59 mm for random selection** across 11 held-out families ([arXiv:2611.18144](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18144)). The restriction is narrow — 3-axis, single-setup, prismatic, no fixturing — and nothing covers 5-axis, sheet-metal bend sequencing, welding or in-process inspection. Cost estimation and quoting have **no measured result at all**, only a vendor claim of ±10% in 90 seconds with no dataset.

## The absences are more informative than the papers

**No learned geometric constraint solver.** In-window systems *reward* constraint satisfaction (ConCAD), *extract* it as a metric (G-CSR, CIT-CAD), or *call* the kernel's solver through an agent-ready API (ReliCAD). No paper proposes a learned or hybrid constraint solver, and sketching remains downstream of 2D→program generation. Since constraint recovery is the bottleneck for editing — HistCAD's 29% industrial edit success is a constraint-prediction number — this is the largest unclaimed prize in the field.

**No meta-evaluation.** The absence is narrower than it looks: general CAD surveys did appear, and the survey row tripled (10→13→31 by regex), including a critical review of engineering-grade parametric CAD and a systematic review of foundation models in reverse engineering. What does not exist is a survey or audit **of CAD evaluation itself**; the last general CAD-ML survey predates the window (2024, [arXiv:2402.17695](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17695)). The field rebuilt its measurement layer faster than it examined it.

**No contamination audit, and no held-out test server.** The only related finding is the DeepCAD parameter prior; nobody checked pretraining-corpus overlap with ABC/DeepCAD/Fusion splits, and nobody tested whether agents memorised published programs. Leaderboards exist at cadbench.ai, engiworld.github.io and agenticivil.com, but all appear to ship public test sets and self-reported runs; CADWorld explicitly says it is "not a live leaderboard." Parametric CAD Bench also shipped a "v2: A Corrected Benchmark" on 8 September before v3, a warning for anyone citing pre-v3 numbers.

**No field study.** Zero studies measure engineer productivity, review time or error-escape rates in a real workflow. Every deployment claim is a capability benchmark or a first-party anecdote.

**No BOM, cost, version-diff, tolerance-stack-up or CAD-to-CAD translation work.** Bill-of-materials extraction from drawing sets is absent from every dataset found; CivilCADBench scores layers, text, dimensions and geometry but not the parts list. Version diffing and change propagation returned nothing. Tolerance stack-up from GD&T has no AI result. STEP AP242 PMI round-trip fidelity between CATIA, NX and SolidWorks is unmeasured despite being the operational reality of every supply chain. Full GD&T semantics — datum reference frames, material condition, tolerance zones bound to faces — have no public evaluation; counting symbols at 59.3% and extracting numbers at 0.963 F1 are proxies. Assembly benchmarks remain toy-scale (6 and 16 assemblies for mates, 25 for RCB-Assm25, 602 polycubes for SixthView), and none uses a 500-part production assembly or dirty supplier geometry.

Also absent: no learned process reward model, no DPO result, no adversarial reward-hacking study beyond the pre-window CAD-Judge warning, no human-preference correlation for any proposed metric, and licences unstated for nearly every benchmark. No new ABC or MFCAD++ release appeared either, so shared-split risk persists.

## What to watch next quarter

**A validity-oracle fight, and possibly a first held-out server.** The tolerant-B-rep result gives anyone with a kernel a way to test whether "valid" means anything, and DrawScore gives anyone with a drawing a ground-truth-free check. The obvious next move is a benchmark that adopts one as a gate rather than a metric. Watch CAD Bench and the ECCV workshop lineage: both already keep private grader assets, so both could ship the first genuinely held-out CAD split. If neither does, contamination stays unaddressed for another quarter.

**The constraint solver.** Everything stuck — 29% industrial edit success, 37% instruction-guided editing, 17.5% GUI work — bottoms out in recovering and solving geometric constraints from geometry and intent. A learned or hybrid 2D/3D constraint solver is the highest-leverage unpublished result available, and the window's production of constraint *rewards* and constraint *metrics* without a solver suggests the problem is recognised and not yet cracked. Expect a first attempt within two quarters, evaluated on HistCAD.

**Reward hierarchy escaping CAD.** PhyCAD's finding — that flat multi-objective GRPO lets one criterion compensate for another, so adding physics *lowers* joint feasibility — is a statement about verifiable-reward RL generally. It should change how every engineering-agent paper aggregates heterogeneous verifiers, and the test of whether the field learned it is whether 2027 papers stop reporting a single "success rate" over geometrically and physically verified rollouts.

**Vendor previews meeting independent measurement.** AutoTimeline is in preview, mate prediction is at 65–75%, and HistCAD says 29% of industrial constraint predictions support the requested edit. Someone will audit AutoTimeline against a held-out set of imported parts, and that will be the first real calibration of a shipped CAD-AI feature. Watch also whether the Schneider–PTC deal closes and whether "design data as substrate" produces a product rather than a slide.

**The near-duplicate stream thinning.** The falsifiable prediction: the text-to-CAD paper count keeps rising while the *share* reporting a result no other paper reports falls, because the cost of entry has collapsed and the cost of a defensible evaluation has not. The signal is the ratio of new benchmarks to peer-reviewed ones — 29 to 5 by that same secondary census — and whether the ECCV workshop's hidden test set produces a second generation of papers reporting against a shared, non-public split. That, not another thirty `*-CAD` papers, would be evidence the field is maturing.

## Conclusion

Read this window as an imbalance: the cost of producing a CAD result collapsed while the cost of *checking* one did not. That single fact explains 199 preprints against four NeurIPS acceptances, thirty-five papers with the same title pattern, and a measurement literature that spent the quarter demonstrating the previous five years of IoU numbers were integrating away the only errors engineers care about. The capability that exists is narrower and more useful than the narrative — a propose-then-verify agent around a deterministic kernel that can reconstruct prismatic features, propose mates, plan 3-axis machining and draft drawing views under human sign-off, while failing at roughly two-thirds of edits and 96% of cross-application workflows.

The strategic implication is that CAD AI will arrive as a **review accelerator before it arrives as an author**. That is not a consolation prize: every measured win in this window came from closing the loop — measure-then-claim, simulation-graded redesign, formability as a representation constraint — and every one of them is a harness, not a model. The organisations that win the next year will be the ones that own the verifier, because in a field where the model is a proposal generator, the oracle is the product.
